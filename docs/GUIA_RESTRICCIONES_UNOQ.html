Checklist: the limits we must meet

  1. ≤ 60 s per problem (TIME_LIMIT_SECONDS=60). The request times out at about 57 s, because copilot/pipeline.py keeps a 3 s margin (MARGIN = 3.0). On a timeout, predict.py answers D with status timeout. Official
  2. One letter A–E per problem, forced with the grammar root ::= [A-E]. Official
  3. Offline: everything runs on the board, with no network (internet only in setup steps 2–4). Official
  4. Deterministic: temperature 0 and SEED=42. The same answers every time. Official
  5. 4 GB of RAM (3.6 GiB visible), shared by Debian, App Lab (Arduino's app tool), the model services and our code. Official
  6. CPU only: llama.cpp pinned at commit 2cbe0ec, 4 threads, no GPU and no NPU. Official
  7. Beat the baseline's 22.5 %, and above all the 25 % of always answering D. Measured (organizers')
  8. No external modules: the 8×13 LED matrix is the only hardware output. Official
  9. M3Kang data is never published (research-only license). Not in the repo, and not in the Git history. Rules: CLAUDE.md. Official
  10. Live demo + 3-minute pitch, with Wi-Fi off and the services started at boot. Official

1. Official challenge rules

The challenge statement: design and build a math copilot that runs entirely on the Arduino UNO Q and uses M3Kang in an innovative way to help students reason mathematically. The copilot must meet one of these two goals:

Teach

Guide the student without giving the answer: hints, adaptive practice, error analysis, physical interaction, collaboration. Judged on originality and measurement.

Reason

Pick the correct answer to a Kangaroo problem (Cangur in Catalan). The organizers measure it objectively on their hidden test. They score every team's predict.py there, whatever the team builds.

Careful with n = 40. With p = 0.225, the standard error is √(0.225·0.775/40) ≈ ±6.6 pp (percentage points). The 95 % confidence interval is about ±13 pp. So 22.5 %, 25 % and 17.5 % are not statistically different. Budget formula

T ≈ Temb + P / 10.1 + N / 4.8  ≤ 57 s
  • Temb = time to embed the question and search the index. It applies only with RAG.
  • RAG (retrieval) adds the most similar solved problems to the prompt as examples.
  • P = input tokens (instruction + problem + options + RAG examples). N = generated tokens.
  • With N = 1, about 570 prompt tokens fit. Calculated
  • With 2 RAG examples, the time goes from about 9 s to about 30 s (organizers' measurement). At the organizers' rate of about 11 tok/s, the prompt grows by about 240 tokens, about 120 tokens per example. Calculated

6. State of the art

M3Kang (Torres-Camps et al., Qualcomm, 2026): accuracy in English

1,747 unique Kangaroo problems, 108 languages, 111,198 problems after filtering. Mean of 3 runs. VLMs (vision-language models) see the image. Table 2 of the paper, arXiv 2601.16218. The paper groups languages into high-, mid- and low-resource by how much text in each language is on the internet. Paper

large / closed models open models ≤ 14B our baseline on the board random guess 20 %
ModelEnglishMean over languagesHigh-resourceMidLow
Not comparable 1:1. The paper evaluates VLMs that see the image, with free-form prompts. We use OCR text (options in about 62 % of cases), a one-letter grammar, and a 0.8B model on a CPU with 60 s. Use it as a reference ceiling, not as a target.

Paper findings that help us

  • Gemma 1B/4B/12B score below random because they do not follow the output format. Our [A-E] grammar removes that failure. Paper
  • Small 3–7B models are near random in low-resource languages (Qwen-2.5-3B: 30.3 % in English, 19.4 % in low-resource languages). Mid-resource languages, Catalan's group, stay close to high-resource ones. Paper
  • MTR (Model Tongue Reasoning) gives the model the question and its English translation. It cuts the gap between high- and low-resource languages from 7.1 to 2.5 pp (Qwen-2.5-3B) and from 12.3 to 3.1 pp (7B). Accuracy comes close to the English level. This fits with using the shared M3Kang id to search in English. Paper
  • Text beats figures for every model and every level. Humans show the opposite. Paper
  • Human difficulty does not predict model difficulty: Kimi-Thinking is above the 94th percentile at levels 3–6, but only at the 47th–74th at levels 0–2 (English). Paper
  • Human baseline: more than 68,000 students aged 10–18 from Kangaroo 2024 in Catalonia, 114 problems. Paper

Another Kangaroo benchmark

Igualde-Sáez et al. 2025 (arXiv 2506.07418): Kangaroo 2014–2024 in English, French, Spanish and Catalan. Gemini 2.0 Flash is the best on problems with images, followed by Qwen-VL 2.5 72B and GPT-4o. No model comes close to the human level. The Catalan versus Spanish result depends on the model family. Paper

8. Open questions

  • ⚠️ Is there an official rubric? How much does the demo weigh against the repo and the hidden test?
  • ⚠️ Are there limits on model size, external data or synthetic data?
  • ⚠️ In which language(s) must the copilot answer? Which languages are in the hidden test?
  • ⚠️ Do llama.cpp's OpenCL or Vulkan backends work on the Adreno 702, and are they faster than the CPU? Optional experiment: do not change the contest build.
  • ⚠️ Power in watts, and thermal throttling during a long evaluation (/sys/class/thermal/*/temp).
  • ⚠️ The real speedup of Q4_0 / Q4_K_M over Q8_0 on this board, and its cost in accuracy.
  • ⚠️ Does memory bandwidth or compute limit decode on this CPU? Not measured on our board.
  • ⚠️ Does cache_prompt reuse the prompt that the server already read, while the services run with --cache-ram 0? Not measured on our board.

9. What to try

  • Interaction with cache_prompt, a server option that keeps the prompt it already read (the organizers' tip, BOARD_SETUP §13.2). Send the problem to the language model as soon as it is on screen. When the student asks for help, the model reads only the new tokens. Read prompt_n in the server's timings: it is the number of tokens that the model really read.
  • Rewrites of 50–100 tokens take 10–20 s. Make them ahead of time, not while the student waits, and store them in SQLite (BOARD_SETUP §13.2).
  • Measure our speed with llama-bench. BOARD_SETUP step 3 builds only llama-server and llama-cli, so build llama-bench first.
  • Claim an improvement on the full validation set: python3 eval.py --limit 0 (about 45 min for 281 problems).
