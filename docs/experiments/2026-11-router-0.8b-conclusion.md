# Intent router: conclusion of the 0.8B classifier comparison

This page records what the team decided after two rounds of comparing three
0.8B classifiers for the tutor's intent router. The round reports hold the
full tables:
[round 1](2026-11-router-bakeoff.md) (100 messages) and
[round 2](2026-11-router-bakeoff-2.md) (150 new messages).

## Decision

**decider-0.8b, with the `plain` prompt format and the canonical option
order, is the tutor's intent router. Two deterministic rules run before it:**

1. keywords for "asks for the solution", in Catalan, Spanish and English
2. a pattern for several option letters in one message ("A? B? C?").

This is a team decision that **departs from the pre-registered outcome**. In
both rounds the rule said "do not adopt", because recall on one label was
below 60 %. The reasons for the override are below. The decision depends on
a test on the board, which has not run yet.

**Update after round 3.** [Round 3](2026-11-router-bakeoff-3.md) compared
decider-2b Q4_K_M with decider-0.8b on the same 250 messages. decider-2b
passed its rule (87.6 % against 80.8 %, p = 0.009), so it replaces
decider-0.8b as the router to try on the board. Its gain is almost all on
"solution" messages. On the messages that do not go to rules, the two models
are equal. To set it up, see
[How to install and use decider-2b as the intent router](../how-to/use-decider-2b-router.md).

## What was compared

The router labels the student's message with one of five labels, proposed in
[the tutor response policy](../explanation/tutor-response-policy.md#labels-for-the-intent-router):
stuck, concept, attempt, solution and not_productive. Rules then pick the
tutor's response type.

| Model | Source | Role |
|---|---|---|
| Qwen3.5-0.8B | `bartowski/Qwen_Qwen3.5-0.8B-GGUF` | Already loaded on the board, so no extra RAM |
| Intern-Decision-0.8B | `internlm/Intern-Decision-0.8B`, revision `42457fa`, own conversion | Decision model fine-tuned from Qwen3.5-0.8B |
| decider-0.8b | `Mapika/decider-0.8b`, own conversion | Decision model, English only by its model card |

All three are Q8_0 GGUFs of the same architecture, served by llama.cpp
`2cbe0ec` on the laptop CPU. Each model ran with two prompt formats (`plain`
and `intern`) and two option orders. The readout is the next-token
probability of the letters A to E. The messages are invented, in Catalan,
Spanish and English, and contain no M3Kang text.

## Results across both rounds

Accuracy with the canonical option order, best format of each model. The
majority-label floor is 20 %.

| Model / format | Round 1 (n = 100) | Round 2 (n = 150) | Lowest recall on one label | McNemar against Qwen3.5 |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Qwen3.5 / `intern` | 39 % | 36.7 % | 0 % (solution, not_productive) | Reference |
| Intern-Decision / `intern` | 51 % | Not rerun | 0 % (solution, not_productive) | 16 vs 4, p = 0.012 (round 1) |
| decider / `plain` | **84 %** | **78.7 %** | 60 % (round 1), 37 % (round 2), both on solution | 45 vs 0 (round 1), 63 vs 0 (round 2), p < 0.001 |

In the McNemar column, the first number counts messages that only the model
gets right and the second counts messages that only Qwen3.5 gets right.

## Why each model won or lost

- **Qwen3.5-0.8B.** With `plain` it answers A for every message. With
  `intern` it labels most messages "attempt" and never finds "solution" or
  "not_productive".
- **Intern-Decision-0.8B.** It beats Qwen3.5 by 12 pp but labels almost all
  "solution" and "not_productive" messages as "stuck". The round-1 rule
  rejected it, so round 2 did not rerun it.
- **decider-0.8b.** It is the best in both rounds. Its errors gather in two
  places: "solution" messages in Catalan and Spanish (2 of 10 right in each
  language in round 2, against 7 of 10 in English), and messages with
  several letters, which it labels "attempt".

## Why the team overrode the rule

- **Consistent:** 84 % in round 1 and 78.7 % on new messages in round 2.
- **Far ahead:** no message in either round is right for Qwen3.5 and wrong
  for decider-0.8b.
- **Stable:** its label changes on 10 of 100 and 24 of 150 messages when the
  option order changes, against 27 and 60 for Qwen3.5.
- **Works in Catalan:** 74 % (37 of 50) in round 2, despite its model card.
- **The failed labels already go to rules.** The tutor response policy sends
  "asks for the solution" to keyword rules and "several letters" to pattern
  rules before any model runs. The two rules above cover the two weak spots.

## Conditions and open risks

- **RAM on the board.** A second model costs about 0.8 GB at Q8_0 or about
  0.5 GB at Q4_0, on top of about 1.1 GB already in use, out of about 3.6 GiB.
- **Latency.** About 1.0 s per call on the laptop. Not measured on the
  board's A53 CPU.
- **Quantization.** Q4_0 is not tested. For Intern-Decision, Q8_0 already
  moved close calls by up to 0.11 in probability.
- **The rules.** The keyword and letter rules are not written or tested yet.
- **Data.** One author (Claude) wrote all messages and labels. The messages
  are short and clean, and many round-2 messages paraphrase round-1 forms. No
  real student message was used.
- **Prompt.** The `plain` layout and the label descriptions follow decider's
  own prompt format, which favours it.

## Next steps

1. Write the two rules and test them on the "solution" and "several letters"
   messages of both rounds.
2. Quantize decider-0.8b to Q4_0 and measure RAM, latency and accuracy on the
   UNO Q with the round-2 messages.
3. Label a sample of real student messages, or have a second person label
   the existing ones.
